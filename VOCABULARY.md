# Vocabulary

Terminology used throughout Theorem Terminal.

## Context

The persistent mathematical environment maintained by Theorem Terminal.

Context records what has been learned, where ideas came from, which claims
have been checked, which explanations are human or machine-generated, and what
needs review.

The term does not imply a hierarchy of mathematical knowledge. It describes
the surrounding information needed to work effectively.

## Aspect

An independent dimension of mathematical information.

Examples:
- formal verification;
- mathematical understanding;
- learning state.

Aspects may interact but are not ordered as layers.

## Representation

A form in which a mathematical object appears.

Examples:
- orientation;
- informal explanation;
- theorem statement;
- proof outline;
- proof skeleton;
- formal statement;
- formal proof.

Representations form a study trajectory, not a dependency chain.

## Understanding field

An attributed record of what a mathematician understands, explicitly does not
understand, or is still trying to determine about a mathematical object.

Its epistemic state may be unknown, question, partial, or accepted. A correction
creates a new field that supersedes the earlier one; it does not overwrite the
earlier record.

## Provenance

The origin and attribution of a claim or artifact.

Examples:

- human judgment;
- AI proposal;
- machine extraction;
- source quotation.

Adoption of an AI proposal does not change its origin.

## AI-generated mathematical content

A theorem, proof, explanation, example, or other mathematical representation
produced by a model.

Content produced outside Theorem Terminal may be imported through a source
occurrence and studied like any other representation. Formal verification does
not turn its origin into human judgment.

## System-generated assistance

An AI proposal produced by Theorem Terminal.

It is one source of AI-generated mathematical content, not a condition for
studying such content. AI output produced elsewhere may be imported and studied
before Theorem Terminal generates any assistance.

## Mastery

The retained understanding of a mathematical object at a particular level.

Examples:
- orientation;
- statement;
- dependencies;
- proof idea;
- technical details;
- formal proof.

Mastery is distinct from verification and from scheduling.

## Correspondence

A provenance-bearing claim that two source occurrences are related.

Examples:
- formalizes;
- restates;
- strengthens;
- weakens;
- approximates.

Correspondence is a mathematical judgment, not a lookup.

## Verification

An attestation that a claim has been checked by a specified method.

Examples:
- Lean kernel verification;
- external checker;
- peer review;
- independent reproduction.

Verification does not establish understanding.

## Discrepancy

A recorded conflict between claims, sources, or generated facts.

A discrepancy is information to investigate, not merely an error condition.

Examples:
- stale correspondence;
- changed signature;
- manuscript/formalization mismatch.
