# Two calculations of a quadratic discriminant

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ and $\beta=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. The field trace
$\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(a)$ is the sum of $a$ and its conjugate, obtained by replacing $\sqrt5$ by $-\sqrt5$.
For a basis $(b_1,b_2)$ of $K$ over $\mathbb Q$, its discriminant is
$\det(\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(b_i b_j))_{i,j=1}^2$.

The [trace notebook](trace.ipynb) uses the basis $(1,\beta)$. Since $\beta^2=\beta+1$,
its trace matrix is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its determinant is $5$.

The [resultant notebook](resultant.ipynb) uses the minimal polynomial $p(t)=t^2-t-1$ of $\beta$; this quadratic is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ because it has no rational root.
The resultant $\mathrm{Res}(p,p')$ is the determinant of the Sylvester
matrix of $p$ and its derivative. For a monic quadratic, the discriminant of
its root basis is $-\mathrm{Res}(p,p')$. This calculation also gives $5$.
These are two methods for the same basis, not two independent proofs of the
resultant identity.

The choice of basis matters. The basis $(1,\sqrt5)$ instead has trace matrix
$\mathrm{diag}(2,10)$ and discriminant $20$. Thus comparing the two
notebooks requires checking their basis as well as their numerical outputs.

The [failed notebook](failed.ipynb) tries to construct a number field from $(t-1)^2$. Sage
rejects this reducible polynomial. A zero polynomial discriminant is not the
discriminant of a quadratic number field.

## Which discriminant belongs to the field?

The two bases span the same rational vector space but different integer
lattices. The [integral-basis argument](integral-basis.md#the-ring-of-integers)
determines the full ring of integers and explains why the field discriminant is
$5$. The determinant $20$ belongs to the smaller lattice generated by
$1$ and $\sqrt5$.
